The official gazetted South African Revenue Service (SARS) filing season dates for the 2024, 2025, and 2026 years of assessment—along with the statutory rules governing provisional tax payments under the Fourth Schedule to the Income Tax Act, No. 58 of 1962—are documented below.

### Key Statutory Filing Notes

* **Auto-Assessment Requests for Reduced or Additional Assessment:** Under Section 95(6) extension notices (such as GN 4918 for 2024, GN 6390 for 2025, and GN 7602 for 2026), taxpayers issued an automatic assessment who wish to amend their return have until the close of the non-provisional filing window (21 October 2024, 20 October 2025, or 23 October 2026, respectively) or 40 business days from the date of the assessment notice if issued late. For the 2026 tax year, SARS expanded the auto-assessment program to include eligible provisional taxpayers.
* **Trusts (ITR12T) & Third-Party Declarations:** Both provisional and non-provisional trusts share the final January submission deadline (20 January 2025 for 2024, 19 January 2026 for 2025, and 22 January 2027 for 2026). Note that for 2025, GN 6217 and the July Practitioner Connect specified 19 September 2025 as the opening date, while the October SARS Digest referenced 20 September 2025. Trustees must also submit the mandatory IT3(t) third-party data return by 30 September following the tax year-end.

## 2. Statutory Rules for Provisional Tax & 3rd Top-Up Payments

### Statutory Distinction: Paragraph 21 vs. Paragraph 23A of the Fourth Schedule

While commonly grouped together under provisional tax obligations for individuals and trusts, the Fourth Schedule to the Income Tax Act separates mandatory in-year provisional payments from the voluntary post-year-end top-up payment:

* **Paragraph 21 (Payment of Provisional Tax by Provisional Taxpayers Other Than Companies):** Strictly governs the two **mandatory** provisional tax periods for natural persons and trusts:
* **First Period — Paragraph 21(1)(a):** Due within six months of the commencement of the year of assessment (on or before 31 August for a standard February year-end). Calculated as half of the total estimated normal tax liability for the year, less applicable primary/secondary/tertiary rebates, medical scheme fees tax credits (Section 6A/6B), and employees' tax (PAYE) deducted during the six-month period.
* **Second Period — Paragraph 21(1)(b):** Due no later than the last day of the year of assessment (28/29 February). Calculated as the total estimated normal tax liability for the full year (after rebates and medical credits), less total PAYE for the year and the amount actually paid for the first provisional period.


* **Paragraph 23A (Additional Provisional Tax Payments):** Explicitly governs the **voluntary third provisional ("top-up") payment** made on an IRP6(3) return after the end of the year of assessment, read together with **Section 89quat** of the Income Tax Act. (Note: Paragraph 23 governs mandatory payments for companies).

### Statutory Mechanics of the 3rd Top-Up Payment (Paragraph 23A & Section 89quat)

1. **Purpose & Section 89quat(2) Interest Mitigation:** Under Paragraph 23A(1), any provisional taxpayer may, for the purpose of avoiding or reducing interest liability under Section 89quat, elect to make an additional payment of provisional tax in respect of any year of assessment. Section 89quat(2) levies non-deductible interest on the "underpayment" (shortfall between final assessed normal tax and total "credit amount" consisting of PAYE, foreign tax credits, and 1st, 2nd, and 3rd provisional payments) where normal tax exceeds the credit amount and—in the case of a natural person or trust—taxable income exceeds **R50,000** (or R20,000 for companies).
2. **Statutory Deadline ("Effective Date" under Section 89quat(1)):** To qualify as a credit that prevents Section 89quat(2) interest from accruing, the third payment must be made on or before the statutory **effective date**:
* **February Year-End (All Individuals, Trusts, and February-Year-End Companies):** Within **seven months** after the close of the year of assessment, meaning on or before **30 September** (or the last business day of September if 30 September falls on a weekend or public holiday).
* **Approved Non-February Year-End (Other Companies or Persons with Section 66(13A)/(13C) Approval):** Within **six months** after the last day of that year of assessment.
* If a top-up is not paid by 30 September, Section 89quat(2) interest runs from **1 October** (the day following the effective date) up to the day before the first due date reflected on the ITA34 Notice of Assessment.


3. **Calculation Formula:** Unlike the 1st and 2nd periods, which rely on estimates, the 3rd top-up is based on actual computed taxable income for the closed tax year:

$$\text{3rd Provisional Payment} = \text{Actual Normal Tax Liability (net of Section 6, 6A, 6B rebates/credits)} - \text{Full-Year PAYE} - \text{Section 6quat Foreign Tax Credits} - (\text{1st IRP6 Paid} + \text{2nd IRP6 Paid})$$


4. **No Relief from Paragraph 20 or Paragraph 27 Penalties:** Making a Paragraph 23A third provisional top-up payment **only** eliminates or reduces Section 89quat interest. Statutory provisions explicitly exclude the 3rd payment from curing:
* **Paragraph 20 Underestimation Penalty (20%):** Calculated strictly on the 2nd provisional estimate submitted by the end of the year of assessment (or within four months thereafter under Paragraph 20(2C), after which a deemed nil estimate applies). Paying a 3rd top-up in September does not reduce the 20% underestimation penalty if the February IRP6(2) fell below the statutory safe-harbour thresholds (80% of actual taxable income if over R1 million, or the lower of 90% of actual taxable income and the "basic amount" if R1 million or less).
* **Paragraph 27(1) Late Payment Penalty (10%):** Applies strictly to late payments of the 1st (Paragraph 21(1)(a)) and 2nd (Paragraph 21(1)(b)) provisional periods. Because the 3rd payment is voluntary, no 10% late payment penalty applies if you do not submit or pay an IRP6(3).
