Annual return fees, filing windows, and beneficial ownership (BO) obligations at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) are governed by distinct statutory regimes for Companies and Close Corporations.

---

## 1. Official CIPC Annual Return Fee Tiers

### Companies (Companies Act 71 of 2008)

Under **Section 33(1)** of the Companies Act, 2008, read with **Regulation 30(1)** and **Annexure 2, Table CR 2B** of the Companies Regulations, 2011, every category of company (including private, public, personal liability, state-owned, non-profit, and external companies) must pay an annual return fee scaled by its annual turnover from its latest approved financial statements.

For companies, the late fee **replaces** the standard on-time fee rather than adding onto it:

| Annual Turnover Tier | Filed On Time (Within 30 Business Days) | Filed Late (After 30 Business Days) | Statutory Form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Less than R1 million** (including dormant) | R100 | R150 | CoR 30.1 (or CoR 30.3 for external) |
| **R1 million to less than R10 million** | R450 | R600 | CoR 30.1 |
| **R10 million to less than R25 million** | R2,000 | R2,500 | CoR 30.1 |
| **R25 million or more** | R3,000 | R4,000 | CoR 30.1 |

* **Dormant Companies:** Neither the Companies Act nor CIPC administrative practice exempts dormant companies from filing. Zero-turnover companies are billed at the lowest tier (R100 on time / R150 late), though **Regulation 30(8)** permits a company that was inactive during the preceding financial year to apply for a fee exemption supported by financial statements showing zero turnover.
* **Turnover Validation:** CIPC cross-checks declared turnover against revenue figures submitted in iXBRL Annual Financial Statements (AFS) or Financial Accountability Supplements (Form CoR 30.2).

### Close Corporations (Close Corporations Act 69 of 1984)

Under **Section 15A** of the Close Corporations Act, 1984, read with **Regulation 16** of the Close Corporations Administrative Regulations, Close Corporations operate on a two-tier schedule where a flat **R150 penalty is added** to the base fee for each late return:

| Annual Turnover Tier | Filed On Time (Within 2-Month Window) | Late Penalty Added | Total Late Fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **R0 to R50 million** | R100 | R150 | R250 |
| **R50 million and above** | R4,000 | R150 | R4,150 |

* **Reinstatement Fee:** If either a Company or a Close Corporation is finally deregistered for missing two or more consecutive annual returns under **Section 82(3)** of the Companies Act, the statutory fee to apply for reinstatement (Form CoR 40.5) under **Section 82(4)** is **R200**, after which all outstanding annual returns must be paid at the applicable late rates within 30 business days.

## 2. Filing Window: Anniversary Date vs. Month Start

The trigger date for the filing clock depends strictly on whether the entity is incorporated as a **Company** or a **Close Corporation**:

* **Companies (Starts from Exact Anniversary Date):** Under **Regulation 30(1)(a)** of the Companies Regulations, 2011, a company must file its annual return within **30 business days after the anniversary of its date of incorporation** (or the date its registration was transferred to South Africa). Under **Section 5(3)** of the Companies Act, counting excludes the anniversary day itself, includes the 30th day, and excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and official South African public holidays. It does **not** start from the beginning of the month or the financial year-end.
* **Close Corporations (Starts from the First Day of the Anniversary Month):** Under **Section 15A** of the Close Corporations Act and **Regulation 16** of the Close Corporations Administrative Regulations, a Close Corporation does **not** use the 30-business-day rule. Instead, its filing window opens on the **first day of its anniversary month and closes at the end of the following calendar month** (a two-calendar-month window). For example, a Close Corporation incorporated on 18 March can file without penalty anytime from 1 March to 30 April each year.

## 3. Mandatory Beneficial Ownership Filing Triggers

The **General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022** amended the Companies Act (specifically **Sections 1, 33, 50, 56, and 69**) and the Close Corporations Act, read with the **Companies Amendment Regulations, 2023** (gazetted 24 May 2023), establishing a mandatory Beneficial Ownership (BO) register at CIPC.

### Statutory Categorization: Affected vs. Non-Affected Companies

Before filing, an entity must classify itself under **Section 1** of the Companies Act:

* **Affected Company:** A regulated company under **Section 117(1)(i)**—namely a public company (listed or unlisted), a state-owned company (unless exempt), or a private company where more than 10% of issued securities were transferred between unrelated persons within the preceding 24 months (or whose MOI opts into Parts B and C of Chapter 5)—as well as any private company that is a subsidiary of or controlled by a regulated company. Affected companies maintain a register of disclosures of beneficial interest under **Section 56(7)(aA)** and **Regulation 32A**.
* **Non-Affected Company:** Every other company (including typical owner-managed private companies, non-profit companies, and Close Corporations), which must record beneficial owners in its securities or members register under **Section 50(3A)** and **Regulation 32(3)(b)**.

### Ownership and Control Threshold Triggers

A **beneficial owner** (**Section 1**) is always a natural person ("warm body") who, directly or indirectly, ultimately owns the company or exercises effective control over it:

* **The 5% Ownership / Interest Trigger:** Under **Regulation 32A(1)(a)** (for affected companies) and CIPC's binding administrative enforcement under **Regulation 32(3)(b)** (for non-affected companies and Close Corporations), any natural person who directly or indirectly holds **5% or more** of the beneficial interest in a class of securities, shareholding, members' interest, or voting rights must be declared. Where shares are held by a juristic person, partnership, or trust, the filer must pierce the corporate veil and report the natural persons behind the chain (including trust founders, trustees, and named beneficiaries).
* **Effective Control Trigger (0% Ownership):** Even if no individual holds 5% or more, any natural person who exercises effective control must be declared. Under **Section 1**, this includes the right to appoint or remove a majority of directors, control over voting rights via shareholder agreements, or the ability to materially influence the management of the entity (including board members of Non-Profit Companies without voting members).

### Event and Timeframe Triggers for CIPC Submission

Filing a Beneficial Ownership declaration carries **no CIPC fee** (Annexure 1, Table CR 1 prescribes "No fee to be charged" for Forms CoR 32B and CoR 121A), but strict statutory deadlines apply:

1. **New Incorporations (10 Business Days):** Under CIPC transitional rules and **Regulation 32B**, any company Incorporated on or after 24 May 2023 must file its initial Beneficial Ownership declaration within **10 business days** after the date of incorporation.
2. **Changes in Beneficial Ownership (Two-Step 10-Business-Day Clock):**
* *Step 1 (Internal Register Update):* Under **Regulation 32(3A)** (non-affected companies) and **Regulation 32A(2)** (affected companies), the company must update its internal securities or beneficial interest register as soon as practical, but **no later than 10 business days** after receiving notice or becoming aware of any change in beneficial ownership details.
* *Step 2 (CIPC Filing):* Under **Regulation 32B(1)** (and **Regulation 121A** for affected companies), the company must file the applicable notice (Form CoR 32B / CoR 121A) and a copy of the updated register with CIPC within **10 business days** after updating its internal register.


3. **Annual Return Hard-Stop Trigger (Annual Renewal):** Under **Section 33(1)(aA)** and **Regulation 30(1)(b)–(1A)**, every company and Close Corporation must file a copy of its updated securities register and Beneficial Ownership declaration **annually alongside its Annual Return**, even if no changes occurred during the year. Since 1 July 2024 (and reinforced by CIPC Practice Note 1 of 2025), CIPC's electronic portals enforce a **hard programmatic block**: an entity cannot lodge or pay for its Annual Return unless a current Beneficial Ownership declaration for that cycle has been submitted and verified.
